### 2027 Software Engineer Internship Questions : What's something you spend a lot of time thinking about or working on, in any part of your life? What draws you to it?

I spend a significant amount of time thinking about cars, motorcycles, bicycles, planes, rockets, and any other technology that moves things from one place to another.

This obsession began when I was very young, fueled by my Dad's love for cars and motorcycles. When I was younger, he had various motorcycles and a Porsche 911 that I loved riding around in.

Every time I drive a cool car, ride my motorcycle, or fly in a plane, I can't get over the insane level of engineering required to make these vehicles possible. The synchronization of  thousands of individual parts all working in harmony to move something forward as efficiently or quickly as possible is ridiculously cool!

My freshmen year of college, I immediately joined UW Madison's Formula SAE Team (engineering race team). This was a great way for me to work on projects that I found profoundly fascinating. I helped design and construct the wiring harness for both the electric and combustion cars, designed a lap timer system for testing, and assembled a PCB for meeting brake light safety regulations.

Overall, I consider transportation technology one of the most fascinating (and momentous) advancements in human history, and I can't wait to see what comes next!


### 2027 Software Engineer Internship Questions : Tell us about a challenge, professional or personal, that stretched you. What did you do, and what did you take away from it?

My previous semester at UW Madison was an extremely challenging, yet rewarding, experience that taught me how manage stress, meet deadlines, and achieve excellence.

I enrolled in four of my major's (computer engineering) most difficult classes: Digital System  Design and Synthesis, Computer Architecture, Digital Circuits and Components, and Algorithms. I had multiple people tell me I was insane for doing this and I would end up dropping one of the classes.

I was fighting an uphill battle the entire semester that culminated during the final two weeks—three final projects and four final exams all due within a week. It seemed an insurmountable task; however, I remained steadfast in my work ethic and, little by little, chipped away the challenges layed before me. I finished the semester having completed all of my projects and excelling on all my exams.

This experience taught me that hard work does pay off. Even when others tell us something is impossible, we are far more capable than we give ourselves credit for.
